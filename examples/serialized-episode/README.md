# Example: Ten-Minute Cats — Serialized Episode Contract

Two cats can detect a human decision that will matter within ten minutes. One can briefly interrupt motion; the other can sense the emotional consequence. They live with and care about the humans, so intervention is motivated.

## Serial constraints

- Each episode resolves one immediate choice.
- Season state tracks who knows the cats' abilities, remaining costs, relationships, and consequences.
- The cats may bring a human to a choice, but cannot make it for them.
- Any ability use needs setup, visible rule, cost, and consequence.
- Human emotion remains primary; cat action remains causally necessary.

## Episode manifest requirements

- local goal and payoff;
- season question advanced by one observable fact;
- relationship before/after state;
- ability setup → use → cost;
- pet-human contact or established bond before intervention;
- cold-viewer comprehension without previous-episode exposition dump.
